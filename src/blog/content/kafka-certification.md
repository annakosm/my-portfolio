I've been working as a backend developer for almost 2.5 years, mostly on Spring Boot applications that use Kafka.

For a long time, Kafka was just another tool I used every day. I knew the basics—producers, consumers, and topics—but not much about what was happening behind the scenes. Things like brokers, replication, leader election, and data durability were still somewhat of a black box to me.

That changed last year when I joined a project that relied much more heavily on Kafka. I started digging deeper and quickly realized how much I didn't know. The more I learned, the more I appreciated the power and design of Kafka. At some point, I decided to challenge myself and go for the CCDAK certification.

I studied consistently for about two and a half months using the official book, Confluent's free courses, and a lot of Udemy practice tests. One mistake I made was relying on a few tests that were much easier than the actual exam. They gave me more confidence than they should have. Looking back, I also wish I had spent more time building small Kafka projects instead of only reading and doing practice questions.

The exam environment itself was easier than I expected, especially considering it was an online asynchronous exam. I was quite nervous beforehand because it was my first online certification exam, but the process was smooth. I had to take photos of my room, keep my camera, microphone, and screen sharing active, and make sure my desk was clear except for my laptop and a blank sheet of paper.

The exam questions, however, were more challenging than I expected. The difficulty gradually increased, and the topics covered a mix of theory and real-world scenarios. Some questions focused on performance, replication, troubleshooting, and data loss situations, while others tested core Kafka concepts.

One useful detail is that for multiple-answer questions, the exam tells you exactly how many answers are correct. I mention this because several Udemy practice tests don't do that, which can make them feel harder in a different way.

The biggest benefit of the certification wasn't the certificate itself. It was the confidence I gained. I now feel much more comfortable investigating Kafka-related issues and understanding what might be happening under the hood. In fact, less than a week after passing, I was troubleshooting a Kafka topic mismatch issue and found myself approaching it very differently than I would have a few months earlier.

Overall, if Kafka is part of your daily work—or if you're aiming for a role where Kafka knowledge is important—I think CCDAK is worth it. For me, the biggest benefit was gaining a better understanding of the concepts behind the Kafka features I was already working with in my day-to-day work.
